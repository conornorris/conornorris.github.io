A common concern in debates about immigration policy is the relationship between immigration and crime. Most research shows that immigrants are less crime-prone than natives. However, fears of immigrant-based crime have still driven many anti-immigration policies. In 2010, for example, Arizona adopted SB 1070 which was meant to discourage unauthorized immigrants from living and working in the state. The legislation gave local law enforcement the authority to enforce federal immigration law. One unintended consequence of SB 1070 is that it may reduce the resources that police departments put towards investigating other crimes. In this paper, Conor Norris investigates the effects of this expansion in police authorities on their ability to respond to violent crime. Using data over 18 years, the study examines crime rates in Arizona before and after the passage of SB 1070. The study finds that SB 1070 was associated with an increase in the violent crime rate of around 20 percent, after controlling for other relevant factors. The study suggests that SB 1070 shifted law enforcement’s focus away from violent crimes and towards immigration-related offenses.
